# Making a puzzle, and a rift of them

How to draw a picture in `make.html` that is fun to solve, and how to put
fifteen of them in a rift.

## Four sizes

Size starts a blank board, and Undo brings the old one back. A saved puzzle
keeps its size.

| Size | What it is for |
| :--- | :--- |
| 5×5 | a ten-second puzzle; a symbol, a letter, a face |
| 10×10 | the main game; one thing drawn as a shape |
| 15×15 | every rift of the course after the first; room for detail, a few minutes each, zoomed with two fingers on a phone |
| 20×20 | the owner's alone, for a rift outside the course; a long puzzle, played zoomed on a phone |

The rules below are written for 10×10. On 5×5 nearly every picture is easy,
so aim for a shape worth guessing. On 15×15 and 20×20 keep the same share of
filled squares and let the extra room go to detail, not to a bigger blob.

## What the maker holds you to

- **One answer.** Save stays off until the tag under the board is green,
  when it says how hard the puzzle is. A square outlined in yellow is one the
  numbers cannot decide, so the player would have to guess, and the red tag
  counts them. The maker shows no numbers; Play shows them.
- **A name**, up to 40 letters. It is hidden until the puzzle is solved, so it
  is the reward: name the thing, in one or two words.

## A good picture

1. **Draw one thing, as a shape.** Ten squares across shows an outline, not a
   scene. If it reads as the thing with your eyes half shut, it works.
2. **Fill a third to two thirds of the board.** On 10×10, fewer than 30
   squares leaves the numbers too little to say; more than 70 and the picture
   is a block.
3. **Use the whole board.** An empty row or column is a free line, and so is
   a full one. One or two is a nice start, four makes it a smaller puzzle.
4. **Give it one or two holes**, an eye or a window. They make the picture
   look like something and give the puzzle its hard part.
5. **Break a mirror.** A picture that is the same left and right often leaves
   yellow squares, and the player solves the same half twice. Move or add
   one square on one side and they usually go.
6. **No lone squares scattered about.** A row that reads 1 1 1 tells the
   player almost nothing, and three of them make a guessing game. A thin
   slanted line does the same, since every row it crosses gets a 1.

## A fun puzzle

A good picture is the reward. The fun is in the numbers: the player always
has a next move, and the picture comes out bit by bit.

1. **Give an easy way in.** Two or three lines with a number over half the
   board, 6 or more on 10×10, let the player fill squares at the first look.
2. **Let one thing lead to the next.** Each square found should help a line
   that crosses it. Normal, on the green tag, is the sign of that.
3. **Mix the lines.** Some long runs, and some lines with two or three
   numbers, which are the most fun to work out.
4. **Reveal it slowly.** The player should guess the picture halfway
   through, not at the first look and not only at the end.
5. **Break up a solid block.** There is nothing to work out inside one. A
   notch, a shine spot or a bit that sticks out is where the thinking is.
6. **Play it the next day.** Play, in a rift's panel on Game, tries it. If you were bored or
   stuck, the players will be too.

## The colour picture

Once the tag is green, Colour paints the picture the player sees at the
finish. The toolbar's last button shows the colour in hand; press it for the
sixty inks, pick one and paint any square, filled or empty. The inks
are ten colours and a row of skin, each in five tones from pale to dark,
then five greys from white to black.

- **The toolbar over the board** holds every button, each an icon that
  names itself when you rest on it. At its start are Back and Save, with
  Delete for a saved puzzle. At its end, over the squares, are Undo, Redo
  and Clear, then the step, then the paintbrush, the bucket and the
  eraser, then the colour.
- **The step is one key.** It shows the step the board is in, a pencil for
  Draw or a palette for Colour, with the word beside it where the bar has
  the room. Press it, or Space, to change to the other, as often as it
  takes to compare the two pictures.
- **On a phone** the toolbar is under the board, by your thumb, in two rows
  of large keys, each with its word under its icon: Back, Undo and Redo, and
  Save at the end with Delete before it, then the step, the three tools
  and Clear. The colours stand open under it, a tap each.
- **Paintbrush, Bucket and Eraser** work in Colour. The paintbrush colours
  the squares you tap or drag over. The bucket colours every square joined
  to the one you tap that has its colour. The eraser puts a square back
  plain: black if it is filled, white if it is not.
- **Undo, Redo and Clear** work in both steps. Undo takes back a tap, a
  drag, a fill, Clear or a change of size. Cmd+Z undoes and Shift+Cmd+Z redoes.
- **Exit** goes back to Create, to the rift it came from, and loses nothing: a new picture is kept
  in this browser and is on the board the next time you open the Pixelator.
- **Colour the background too.** A blue sky or a green field behind the shape
  is what makes the finish feel like a reward.
- **Two or three colours are enough.** Keep the shape one colour and use a
  second for its detail, such as a red apple with a green leaf.
- **Shade with one colour's tones.** A deep red down one side of the apple
  and a pale red spot near the top make it round.
- A picture never coloured finishes in black and white.

## The puzzle of the day

Save asks where the puzzle goes. Choose Puzzle of the day, the last choice,
and give it its day in the field that opens; it is that day's puzzle, in no
rift.
A day takes one puzzle, and after a save the day moves on by one, so a week
of them is drawn in one sitting. A day with none gets a shape
the game makes itself, which has no name but its date.

- **Draw ahead.** Nobody is sent a day's puzzle before its day, so next
  week's are safe to save now.
- **Colour it.** Everyone solves the same one on the same day, so its finish
  is the one picture all of them see.
- **Keep it easy or normal.** It is the puzzle people do every day, on a
  phone, often in a spare minute.
- Game lists every day drawn, by date, under Dailies; a tile opens it in
  the maker.

## How hard it is

The green tag says easy, normal or hard. It counts rounds: one round is one
look at every row and every column, filling in what each can now decide.

| It says | Rounds | What it feels like |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| easy | 1 to 3 | long runs; most lines give something on the first look |
| normal | 4 or 5 | a few lines wait for their neighbours |
| hard | 6 or more | the picture comes out one corner at a time |

Under the board a line says how much the first look gives, such as 66% first
look: the share of the board that one look at every row and column decides
before any square is known. A puzzle that gives more is quicker to solve,
and this follows a player's time more closely than the rounds do.

- **To make it easier**, make runs longer: a row of 8 nearly solves itself,
  and a full row solves itself and starts every column.
- **To make it harder**, break long runs into short ones and take out the
  full rows, then check the tag is still green.

## A rift

A rift shows up to fifteen puzzles about one thing, of one size or more,
such as Fruit. On the front page it is one tile: its portal, in the colour
of its rank, beside its name and how much is found. Its own
page letters its puzzles A to O, three across on a phone and five across on
a wide screen. It can hold more, switched off. Its name is on its tile and
heads its page, after its number, and one with no name is called More. Save
asks which one a puzzle goes in: its list counts how many of each rift's are
on, such as Fruit · 15 of 15, 3 off, and offers the first that still has
room. A rift lists its puzzles small boards first and then easy to hard, so
the order they were drawn in does not matter. The last, which is the
hardest, is its boss.

- **No rift yet**, first in that list, keeps the puzzle in Unsorted: a
  section of Game after the rifts. No player is sent a puzzle
  there until it is moved into a rift.
- **New rift**, in that list, starts one, and a field opens for its
  name. It stands after the others and starts hidden, so only the owner
  sees it while its fifteen are drawn.
- **Name it for what is in it**, such as Fruit, Animals or At home. The
  player guesses the picture sooner, and the guess is half the fun. It is
  named as it is started.
- **Draw more than fifteen.** A picture saved into a rift with fifteen on is
  saved off, and no player is sent it.
- **A new picture in a published rift is saved off,** so no player meets it
  half made. Switch it on in the rift's panel when it is ready.

## Game

Game, the switch at the top of Create, is where puzzles and rifts are put
in order. It shows every rift on one page, in order, a block each with its
place, its name, its boards' sizes and its pictures, then Unsorted. A
picture opens it in the Pixelator, and the rift's name opens its panel,
where the arrows and the ticks are. The front page shows only what the
players are sent.

- **The add tile**, after a rift's pictures, opens the Pixelator on a
  blank board of that rift's largest size, and Save offers that rift. The
  Size field changes the board and keeps the rift.
- **Tick puzzles to move them.** Press a tile to tick it, or the box beside
  a rift's name to tick all of it. Move, in the bar above, asks where
  they go: a rift, Unsorted, or a new rift, named there. They arrive on
  while the rift has fewer than fifteen on, and off after that.
- **Delete works on the ticked ones too.** It asks once, and every player's
  best times on them go with them.
- **Swap with the switch under each tile:** switch one off, then a spare on.
  A sixteenth is refused until one is off.
- **Edit** opens a puzzle in the maker. **Play** tries it; for a puzzle no
  player is sent, the time is not kept.
- **Publish a rift** with the switch beside its name: on, every player is
  sent it; off, only Game shows it.
- **Move it with the arrows** in its panel, up or down among the rifts. The
  order is the one players meet them in, and a rift's number is its place.
- **Rename it, or choose who sees it,** from its menu.
- **Delete it from its menu.** Its puzzles are kept: they move to Unsorted,
  and the rifts after it move up. A rift left empty by a move or a
  delete goes the same way.
