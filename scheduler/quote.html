How a quote is priced

  • Under 295 miles in all, a trip is billed a day at a time, at the local daily rate.
  • From 295 miles, it is billed by the mile, with free days. Each day past the free days adds the long distance extra day rate.
  • Dead miles are part of the trip's miles. They are billed at the dead miles rate instead of the mileage rate, and still count toward the 295-mile line and the free days.
  • The regular quote includes one driver. Choosing two adds the second driver's pay, worked out on the trip's miles at the two-driver rates.
  • Every rate and rule is set on the rates page, under Edit rates, including the mile figures on this tab.

Suggested change
1 Crossing 295 miles can lower the price. Under 295 a trip pays for every day. From 295 it pays by the mile with free days, which for a trip of a few days can come to less. Charge whichever is higher: the local days or the mileage price.
2 The daily minimum only applies to local trips. A one-day trip of 295 miles is billed by the mile, which can come to less than one local day. The same change as 1 covers it.
3 An empty last day isn't counted. Days count up to the last day with miles. An empty day in the middle counts; an empty day at the end doesn't. Count the last day when the bus is still out that day.
4 Very long trips drop a charge. From 10,250 trip miles the free-day table runs out and the mileage charge counts as $0. Driver pay does the same from 6,250 miles. Set both "Free days stop at" rules on the rates page past any real trip.
5 The two free-day tables differ. At 750 to 999 miles a quote gets more free days than driver pay does. Keep, if the quote and the driver pay are meant to differ.
6 The meal allowance isn't added to anything. The quote shows it under the second driver's pay, for reference only. Keep, if meals are paid outside the quote. Otherwise add it to the second driver's pay.
7 No miles means no charge. A quote with no miles entered comes to $0 plus other charges. Ask for miles instead of showing a price.
8 Each free-day step can lower the price. From 750 miles every 250 more adds free days, so a trip with extra days dips at each step. Charge at least the price just under each step, or keep the dips as the free days working.
